01 / HIS STORY

From a Small Island to a Nation's Heart

His life demonstrated how curiosity, education and perseverance can turn humble beginnings into an extraordinary national legacy.

01

A journey powered by curiosity

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. Growing up in a modest environment, he developed a strong interest in learning and science. His early experiences helped shape the discipline and determination that would define his life.

01

Early Life & Education

Kalam studied at St. Joseph's College, Tiruchirappalli, before specializing in aeronautical engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology. His fascination with flight and engineering gradually became the foundation for his scientific career.

02

Science & Nation Building

At ISRO, Kalam became closely associated with India's launch-vehicle programme and served as Project Director for SLV-III, which successfully placed the Rohini satellite into near-Earth orbit in 1980. Later at DRDO, he contributed to India's indigenous guided-missile development.

03

Challenges & Responsibility

Kalam's career involved complex technical challenges, ambitious national projects and the responsibility of coordinating large scientific teams. Instead of treating setbacks as final outcomes, he emphasized learning, teamwork and persistent effort.

04

Teacher & People's President

In 2002, Kalam became the 11th President of India. Even while holding the highest constitutional office, he remained deeply connected with students and young people. His focus on education and an ambitious vision for India's future made him widely remembered as the People's President.

Kalam specialized in aeronautical engineering, strengthening his path toward aerospace science.

1969

Kalam moved to ISRO and became closely involved in the development of India's satellite launch-vehicle programme.

SLV-III successfully placed the Rohini satellite into near-Earth orbit, marking an important milestone for India's space programme.

1982
DRDO

Kalam took responsibility for India's indigenous guided-missile development programme.

1997

Bharat Ratna

Kalam received India's highest civilian honour, the Bharat Ratna, recognizing his exceptional contribution to the nation.

2002

Kalam became India's 11th President and served from July 2002 to July 2007.

2015

A Teacher Until the End

Kalam passed away on 27 July 2015 while delivering a lecture at IIM Shillong, leaving behind a lasting educational legacy.
